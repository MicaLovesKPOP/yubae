## Research findings (reported, needs primary review)
- A glyph often consists of a pictographic root (어근) and an upper POS mark: noun horizontal line, verb dot/circle, adjective rising slash, adverb falling slash. Unmarked function words include conjunctions, prepositions, auxiliary forms, plural, numerals, equality and inequality.
- Lower pair of dots creates a colour derivative; documented examples relate ocean/blue, fire/red, salt/white, glass/transparent, charcoal/black. Purple's uncoloured root is unverified.
- An endpoint dot marks an ordinal; reported examples first, second, sixth. Need inspect where dot attaches for each numeral; do not assume dot placement.
- Word order reported SVO/SVC. Verb has/exist (있다) can signal possession/existence/location. Negation can combine can + not-exist. The root of life is visually similar to AND but POS mark distinguishes them; do not merge by geometry.
- 죽다/죽이다 may be intransitive/transitive depending on objects; sound-making is broader than speak; 팔/다리 versus hand/foot requires review.
- Mathematical notation and proper distinction between a table/grid and magic square are needed. The chart's English labels are provisional, not authoritative.
- Stella is reported in the fan wiki to want ongoing use and new words; her own lecture and posts demonstrate active interest. Exact vision/permissions are not independently confirmed. Ask rather than attribute project endorsement.
